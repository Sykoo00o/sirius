FAQ

Got a question?

What exactly is a kegel?

A kegel is a squeeze-and-lift of the pelvic floor, the sling of muscle under your bladder and bowel (and uterus, if you have one). It’s the feeling of holding in wind, then letting go completely. The letting go is half the exercise.

How do I know I’m doing them right?

Your plan opens with a finding-the-muscle session and short cues on every rep: breathe normally, keep your belly, thighs and buttocks soft, and feel a lift inward rather than a push down. If you still can’t feel it after a couple of weeks, a pelvic health physiotherapist can check in person.

How long before I notice anything?

It varies a lot from person to person. Muscle training generally takes weeks of steady practice, and many programmes run for three months. We don’t promise a date; the plan tracks your hold times so you can see your own trend.

Who should check with a doctor first?

Anyone with pelvic pain, pain during sex, blood in urine, a sudden change in bladder or bowel habits, a feeling of heaviness or bulging, or who is pregnant, recently gave birth or recently had pelvic surgery. kegeldoc is general exercise guidance, not medical advice, and no doctor reviews individual plans.
